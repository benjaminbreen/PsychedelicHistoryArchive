AFTERNOON SESSION
Senator BURDICK. Most of our witnesses have been experts in the
field of medicine or other sciences. Because we are concerned with
all the approaches to LSD we are also interested in the position of
those who have used the drug for nonscientific purposes.
Mr. Allen Ginsberg is a poet and attended Columbia University.
He was a Guggenheim fellow during 1965 and 1966. He has read
and published his poems throughout the world, and is one of the
American artists most concerned with the use of LSD-type drugs.
We have heard during these hearings that LSD appeals to many
of our talented young college students; in fact, many of our future
leaders. According to testimony taken here, there is a great deal of
experimentation and use of LSD on college campuses.
For this reason, I think it is important that we hear from the advo-
cates of this drug so that we can better understand why it has such
an attraction for many of our most promising young men and women.
We have been told that, at worst, LSD can be a fatal attraction.
If this is so, we must quickly develop policies regarding its use. Mr.
Ginsberg is one of the most influential advocates of LSD use. He
has spoken on its behalf to many college students across the land.
I feel it is important that he tell this subcommittee of his motives
and experiences so that we can better understand the reasons behind
LSD use and make intelligent decisions on future courses of action
by the Government.
I think it is important that we have on the record the points of
view and the reasoning of these exponents of the drug who are cap-
turing the minds of many of our young people. I do not feel we can
fully understand the problem by ignoring these "Pied Pipers" of the
LSD movement. That is why Mr. Ginsberg is here today.
