Dr. LEARY. I do have a few comments I would like to make,
Senator.

Dr. LEARY. I am grateful for your invitation. I am particularly
grateful and honored to come before this committee, because I think
the most constructive legislation that has been produced in this ad-
mittedly complex field has come from this committee, and I might say
I am very aware of the work done by the chairman of the committee
in constructive remedy to the narcotic drug problem, as well as other
groups attempting to come up with positive answers.
Senator DODD. We have had quite a hard time.
Dr. LEARY. The use of LSD by young people, the occasion of our
deliberations today, has provoked a crisis. I think this is not a crisis
of peril but a crisis of challenge and promise.
Psychedelic drugs are nonaddictive, nontoxic, and antinarcotic.
We don't know exactly what they do. They seem to release neuro-
logical energies.
Now my position is that energy is not dangerous if it is used wisely.
There is nothing to fear in LSD. There is nothing to fear from our
own nervous systems or from our own cellular structures.
On the basis of the statistics so far, I would say there is more vio-
lence, insanity., friction, terror, in the cocktail lounges and barrooms
of any large American city on any one Saturday night than in the
entire 23-year history of LSD use. The so-called peril of LSD re-
sides precisely in its eerie power to release ancient, wise, and I would
even say at times holy sources of energy, which reside inside the human
brain.
Senator DODD. I didn't hear that.
Dr. LEARY. I am suggesting that the energies which are released
which come, of course, from the human brain are ancient energies, I
would say even sacred, because they evolve from our physiological
structure. That is what we are talking about here today, not a drug
but what the drug does in opening up, accelerating, and expanding
consciousness.
Now who is ready or even eager to deal with this eerie power? Ap-
parently, in the United States today it is the young. LSD is fright-
ening, but mainly to those who have not taken the drug. During the
last few months of heated publicity and of occasional bureaucratic
hand wringing about LSD, one simple question has remained unan-
swered. Why? Why are hundreds of thousands of our most intelli-
gent, gifted, best educated young people choosing to expose themselves
to this new and admittedly strange experience?
I have come here today to suggest an answer to this question. My
answer, like the LSD experience itself, may be a stiff dose for those
who are unwilling to look at the record of history. We are faced
today in America, and we are faced today in this room with socio-
biological problems which are rather unique in human history. We
have at hand now energies easily available which are accepted eagerly
by one generation, the young, and which are abhorred by the older

or not, open up reality, like the microscope or the telescope, and, of

are confused or even terrified.
It is interesting that the psychedelic drugs could be, might be the
national antidotes to the addictive narcotic drugs. There his been
